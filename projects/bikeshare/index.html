    Inventory & demand

    The browser reads Capital Bikeshare’s GBFS station information and status feeds at the advertised refresh interval while this page is visible. Live dispatch excludes closed, virtual, unobserved, and stale stations. The clock shows the operator’s update in Washington time.

    Expected net departures come from historical station pickups and returns in local half-hour bins, separated into weekdays and weekends. Stations without a trip-history match still count toward inventory.

    One feasible move

    When either end approaches three bikes or docks, the planner aims to restore a 30% buffer, capped at 12. It protects up to three bikes at pickup and three docks at drop-off and respects truck capacity. Moves rank by buffer restored per crew minute, with priority for an already empty or full station.

    Travel uses 1.3 times straight-line distance at 18 km/h, plus two minutes and 30 seconds per bike at each stop. Weather, traffic, vehicle type, and travel to pickup are outside this estimate.

    Commute replay

    Choose a recorded decision time to run the same planner using only information available then. Reveal the next 30 minutes to compare its one-bike-or-dock forecast alert against a simple three-bike-or-dock inventory alert. Only stations observed at every checkpoint are scored. Already empty or full sides are excluded from new-shortage scoring.

    Business impact

    The replay applies observed inventory changes to both ends of one hypothetical move. Pickup and delivery take effect at the first checkpoint after their estimated times, subject to available stock and space. Empty or full minutes are approximated between samples. Real-world rebalancing and unobserved demand can change the outcome.
